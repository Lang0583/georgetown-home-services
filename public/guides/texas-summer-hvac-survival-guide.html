Texas Summer HVAC Survival Guide

A practical, one-page playbook for Georgetown, TX homeowners—get ahead of triple-digit heat before your AC is under peak load.

Georgetown & Williamson County reality check: Long cooling seasons, afternoon heat indexes past 100°F, and humidity from the Gulf mean your system runs hard for months—not a few weeks. Many neighborhood homes carry 12–18+ year split systems; hard starts, weak capacitors, and dirty coils show up on the first serious heat wave. This guide helps you catch issues in April–May, not July 4th weekend.

10 ways to prep your AC before summer hits

  1. Swap or clean filters on schedule. In Central Texas dust and pollen load filters fast—often every 1–3 months. A choked filter mimics “low refrigerant” with weak airflow and can freeze the coil.
  2. Clear the outdoor condenser. Remove leaves, cottonwood fluff, and vegetation within ~2 feet. Dirty coils can’t shed heat efficiently when Georgetown afternoons bake the yard.
  3. Open and unblock all supply and return grilles. Don’t block returns with furniture or rugs; upstairs returns matter for two-story comfort.
  4. Set realistic thermostat expectations. Many homes stabilize best around 74–78°F when occupied; aggressive setbacks force long afternoon recovery when it’s 102°F outside.
  5. Use ceiling fans correctly. Fans cool people, not thermostats—run them in occupied rooms only so you’re not adding motor heat to an already loaded home.
  6. Flush or verify the condensate line before humidity spikes. Algae and crud in trap lines back up water and can shut the system off—or damage ceilings.
  7. Listen and note the “first hot day” behavior. First 95°F+ day reveals weak capacitors and marginal charge. Write down symptoms (which rooms, what time of day) before you call—that speeds diagnosis.
  8. Check insulation on exposed refrigerant lines (if safely visible). Sun-baked, deteriorated line insulation on the exterior can hurt efficiency; don’t disturb sealed equipment—note it for your tech.
  9. Plan a professional tune-up in spring—not August. A real tune-up checks refrigerant approach, electrical draws, drain safety, and basics you can’t see from the hallway.
  10. Know when to stop DIY. Ice on the system, repeated breaker trips, gas furnace smell (heating season), or major water intrusion—shut it off and call a licensed contractor; don’t chase refrigerant or high-voltage issues yourself.

Warning signs checklist

Check any that apply—if several are true, schedule a licensed HVAC pro before peak summer.

  • AC runs constantly but house won’t get below ~78–80°F on hot afternoons
  • Weak airflow from some vents; rooms never “catch up” upstairs
  • Ice or heavy frost on the outdoor line set or indoor coil area
  • Water pooling near the indoor unit (clogged condensate / safety switch trips)
  • Musty smell, grilles, or temps swing when it storms
  • Breaker trips or burning smell from the air handler closet
  • Thermostat shows “cooling” but air feels room-temperature
  • Unusual grinding, squealing, or buzzing from outdoor condenser
  • Electric bill jumped sharply with no lifestyle change
  • System is 12+ years old and hasn’t had a documented tune-up in 2+ years
