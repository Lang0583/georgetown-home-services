Texas Heat Roof Inspection Checklist

Georgetown, TX — ground-level visuals only. Use binoculars; do not climb unless you are trained and tied off.

Why this matters here: Central Texas sun and attic heat accelerate shingle aging—granules wash off, tabs curl, and seal strips fatigue long before a 30-year label implies. Summer thunderstorms add wind lift at those weak tabs. Checking from the yard after spring lets you document changes before hail season stacks on heat damage.

Heat & UV damage — what to look for

  • Cupping / clawing / curled tab edges — edges turn up or under; often worst on south- and west-facing slopes.
  • Granule loss — bare patches, uneven color, or heavy grit in gutters and downspout splash areas.
  • Flashing gaps or lifting — thin metal at walls, chimneys, or skylights pulling away, cracked sealant, or daylight lines from the ground (use binoculars).
  1. 1. Eaves & gutters — granules and debris

    From the driveway, look for gutter tails full of coarse granules after rain, and whether drip edges look straight. Granule “sands” in one run often map to a worn slope above.

  2. 2. Field shingles — uniform color?

    Scan each visible slope for dark stripes, shiny black asphalt, or blotchy loss of texture—signs the protective granule layer is thin.

  3. 3. Ridge & hip caps

    Caps take the most wind and sun. Look for cracked, missing, or lifted pieces along the top—binoculars help without a ladder.

  4. 4. Wall and chimney flashing (from the ground)

    Step or counter-flashing should lie tight to brick or siding transitions. Note rust streaks, gap shadows, or sealant that has separated.

  5. 5. Vent boots & pipe collars

    Plastic or rubber boots crack in UV; look for ragged collars or lifted skirts around plumbing stacks—common slow-leak sources after heat cycles.

  6. 6. Valleys & upper-story shadows

    Valleys channel water and heat. Look for debris dams, lifted shingles in the channel, or uneven lines where two planes meet.

  7. 7. Interior & attic clues (safe peek)

    After a hard rain, check ceilings for rings or blistering paint. If attic access is safe, use a flashlight—no walking decking if unsure—looking for daylight at penetrations or damp sheathing.

  8. 8. Document with photos & dates

    Wide shots of each elevation plus zoomed problem areas help roofers quote and help insurance timelines if storms hit later—Georgetown’s hail season is real.

Generally OK to DIY

  • Ground & binocular inspection; gutter cleanout from a safe ladder height if you already do it routinely
  • Clearing debris from downspout outlets at grade; trimming branches that touch the roof (carefully)
  • Photos and notes for contractors—no sealant “blob fixes” on steep slopes you can’t reach safely

Call a licensed roofer

  • Active leak, ceiling stain spreading, or storm / hail suspected damage you need documented
  • Widespread curling, major granule loss, or lifted flashing you can see from the ground
  • Any work on steep pitches, second-story edges, or after suspect DIY repairs—falls and moisture traps cost more than a service call
